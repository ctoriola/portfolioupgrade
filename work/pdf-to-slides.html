01The problem

Turning a PDF into a presentation is usually copy, paste, resize, repeat. Converters that exist tend to dump each page onto a slide, or lose the structure: chapter titles, numbered steps, tables and the pictures that go with them.

02How it works

The PDF is converted to Markdown with PyMuPDF4LLM, then a second pass reads the PDF's own font sizes to recover chapter titles, the document title and heading levels the converter gets wrong. Contents, copyright and cover pages are dropped; icons, rules and duplicate images are filtered out.

Each section is then recognised for what it is, a product with a photo and a price, a numbered process, a spec sheet, a table, and given a matching layout: product slide, process slide, stat cards, text and image, gallery. Long sections continue on “(cont.)” slides and chapters get divider slides.

03AI that arranges, not invents

Three slide types: Standard keeps the full text with no AI at all. Condensed and Detailed use an LLM to summarise or expand, with prompts that forbid adding facts, numbers or quotes that aren't in the source. Tables and images are taken out before the call and put back after, so data stays exact.

An optional planner shows the model only an outline and lets it rearrange sections. Every suggestion is checked against the real blocks, and the rule-based plan wins if the model is missing, rate-limited or wrong.

04Textbooks on serverless

Vercel functions stop at 300 seconds and 4.5 MB. So uploads go straight to a private B2 bucket, a textbook is read in 300-page parts four at a time, and the plan travels back to the browser so the server never re-reads the PDF. You tick the chapters you want; only those are built. Scanned pages are OCR'd first.
