01The problem

Turning a PDF into a presentation is usually copy, paste, resize, repeat. Converters that exist tend to dump each page onto a slide, or lose the structure: chapter titles, numbered steps, tables and the pictures that go with them.

02How it works

It reads the document first. A brochure becomes product slides with prices and specs; a handbook becomes chapters, steps and quizzes; a report keeps its tables.

Under the hood the PDF becomes Markdown with PyMuPDF4LLM, then a second pass reads the PDF's own font sizes to recover chapter titles and heading levels the converter gets wrong. Each section is recognised for what it is, a product, a price, a process, a spec sheet, and given a matching layout. Photos, charts and diagrams land on the slide they belong to.

03AI that arranges, not invents

Three slide types: Standard keeps the full text with no AI at all. Condensed and Detailed use an LLM to summarise or expand, with prompts that forbid adding facts, numbers or quotes that aren't in the source. Tables and images are taken out before the call and put back after, so data stays exact.

An AI planner reviews the slide structure from an outline only. Every suggestion is checked against the real content, and the rules win if the model is missing, rate-limited or wrong.

04Textbooks on serverless

Vercel functions stop at 300 seconds and 4.5 MB. So uploads go straight to a private B2 bucket, a textbook is read in 300-page parts four at a time, and the plan travels back to the browser so the server never re-reads the PDF. You tick the chapters you want; only those are built. Scanned pages are OCR'd first, and the PDF is deleted as soon as the deck is built.
